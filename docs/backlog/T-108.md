# T-108: Flavourings and minor animal ingredients do not count as protein

## Goal
Architect decision (2026-11-05), correcting T-104: the protein class is about the main protein of the dish, not about traces. Fish sauce is a flavouring, not fish protein; egg noodles contain egg, but egg is not the protein the dish is built on. Both are `None`, like coconut milk, soy cream and peanut butter (peanuts have protein, but are not the main protein carrier). A dish with fish sauce or egg noodles and no real meat, fish, seafood or other protein is vegetarian at best.

Apply the same principle to the other flavourings the T-104 review found:
- `fiskesaus` = `None` (was `Fish`); also `østerssaus`, `rekepasta`, `ansjospasta` when used as condiments.
- `eggnudler` = `None` (was `AnimalProduct`).
- Seasonings named after an animal are `None`: `kyllingkrydder`, `fiskekrydder`, `kjøttkrydder`, `grillkrydder` (was the animal class).
- Stocks, broths and bouillon (`kyllingbuljong`, `fiskekraft`, `kjøttkraft`, `grønnsakskraft`, `buljongterning`) are flavourings by the same rule: `None` (were `Unknown`). (Orchestrator's reading of the principle; the architect can say otherwise.)
- `soyabiff` = `None` (soy product, was `Beef`); `rognebær` = `None` (a berry, was `Fish`).

Note: `None` means the ingredient adds no protein class, so a recipe with only these and plant food gets food type Vegan or Vegetarian by the T-089 rules. That is the intended "vegetarian at best" result for the meal mix.
